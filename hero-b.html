What is Cub Scouts, really?

If you have never been near Scouting, here is the honest version. What it is, what your child does, and what it asks of you.

It is a small group, by grade

Your child joins a den, a small group of kids in the same grade. The dens together make up the pack.

Kids do things, not sit and listen

Building, cooking, hiking, knots, first aid, looking after a patch of the neighbourhood. The point is learning by doing something slightly harder than last time.

Each year has a name

Kindergarten is Lion, then Tiger, Wolf, Bear, and Webelos across fourth and fifth grade. Each one offers its own things to try.

Grown-ups come too

Cub Scouts is built around families, not drop-off. Grown-ups take part throughout, and nobody is expected to arrive knowing how any of it works.

You do not need to be outdoorsy

Curiosity is enough. You can learn alongside your child, ask questions, and find your footing as you go.

Boys and girls, all welcome

Cub Scouts welcomes boys and girls. Dens are grouped by grade, so kids begin with peers their own age.

Real experiences

What we actually do.

The days look different, but they always give kids something real to try together.

Camping

Set up a tent, share a meal outdoors, and make room for the unfamiliar.

Museum sleepovers

See a museum after its doors close, with a sleeping bag and your den nearby.

Community service

Lend a hand to projects that make a difference around the community.

The Pinewood Derby

Turn a small block of wood into a car, then watch it make its way down the track.

Den meetings

Build, cook, talk, and try things with the kids in your child’s grade.

Hikes and outdoors

Follow a trail, notice what is around you, and see what you can do outside.

The honest questions

Questions new families ask.

Do I have to camp?
No. Camping is one part of Cub Scouts; families choose how they take part.
Does my child need to be outdoorsy?
No. Children are welcome to begin wherever they are, whether they already love the outdoors or are just curious.
Do I have to volunteer?
Families take part throughout. Some roles take more time than others, so talk with the pack about what you can offer.
Can girls join?
Yes. Cub Scouts welcomes girls and boys.
What if we have never done anything like this?
That is okay. No previous Scouting experience is expected, and the first steps are easier when families learn together.
What does my child actually get out of it?
They practice practical skills, get comfortable trying unfamiliar things, and spend time with a small group of peers.
