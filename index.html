What is Cub Scouts, really?

The quick version of what children do and how families take part.

Small groups, familiar faces

Your child joins a den with kids in the same grade. The dens together form the pack.

Learning by doing

Children build, cook, hike, practice first aid, and try something a little harder each time.

Families take part

Cub Scouts is built around families, not drop-off. Grown-ups join in and learn alongside their children.

Boys and girls

Both are welcome, and most families arrive knowing nothing about Scouting.

Simple to start

How it works

  1. Join a den

    Your child starts with kids in their grade.

  2. Dens meet regularly

    Children build skills in a familiar small group.

  3. The pack comes together

    Families share the bigger adventures.

Den meetings
Usually once or twice a month, typically on Sunday afternoons. Each den leader sets their own dates.
Pack meetings
Usually the third Friday of the month, when every den comes together.
Where
In and around Melrose, often at Pine Banks and local parks.

The honest questions

Questions new families ask

Do I have to camp?

No. Camping is one part of Cub Scouts, and families choose how they take part.

Does my child need to be outdoorsy?

No. Curiosity is enough.

Do I have to volunteer?

Families take part throughout. Talk with the pack about what you can offer.

Can girls join?

Yes. Cub Scouts welcomes girls and boys.

What if we have never done this before?

That is okay. No previous Scouting experience is expected.

What does my child get out of it?

Practical skills, confidence trying unfamiliar things, and time with a small group of peers.
